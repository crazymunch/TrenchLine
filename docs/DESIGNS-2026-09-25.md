# Designs after the Trench Companion review

*25 September 2026. Follows [`TRENCH-COMPANION.md`](TRENCH-COMPANION.md),
whose section 7 sets the direction: keep the full app, mirror what Trench
Companion does better, and import from it. This document is the designs
for the parts that need no demo, packed the way the owner asked: fewer,
larger pull requests.*

## Push discipline

The owner, 25 September: "I want to do fewer, larger pull and pushes,
we've overloaded Vercel and Git at various times due to volume of push
and pulls." Every push builds a Vercel preview and runs CI, so from here:

- **One pull request per pack**, and a pack is several designs.
- **One push per pull request.** The branch is pushed once, when the whole
  gate is green locally (`tsc`, lint, the full vitest suite with Postgres,
  `rules:check`). A red CI run is the one reason for a second push. No
  work-in-progress pushes, no "rebase and push" without a change.
- **The developer merges its own pull request** when `check` is green
  (Order 24), and opens the next only after the merge.
- **Documentation rides the pack.** Rule 4 still holds; the doc changes go
  in the same pull request, not a second one.
- The architect's own docs pull requests are at most one a day, and a
  design written while a pack is open waits for the next.

## ADM-1. No built-in admin address

**Finding.** `src/lib/env.ts` carries the owner's personal e-mail address
as the default administrator, with the same address in the tests and in
comments. Anyone reading the repository's public history knows which
account holds admin. Not a credential, and the credentials sweep on 25
September found none in the tree or the history; but an identity is half
of one, and it is the one thing the sweep turned up.

**Change.** The default becomes empty: with no `TRENCHLINE_ADMIN_EMAILS`
set, nobody is an administrator, and `env.ts` says so at startup the way
it does for a missing secret. The address leaves the tests (a fixture
address takes its place) and the comments. `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md` and
`docs/SECURITY.md` record that admin is a deployment setting only.

**Order of operations, in the pull request body in bold.** The owner sets
`TRENCHLINE_ADMIN_EMAILS` in Vercel *before* merging. A deployment that
merges first has no administrator until the variable is set and the site
redeployed; nothing is lost, but the owner should not find out by being
locked out.

## CI-1. Import a warband from a Trench Companion share link

**Why.** A player who builds in Trench Companion and plays here should not
have to rebuild. Their share page carries the full campaign state, which
the NewRecruit import has to reconstruct.

**The source.** A share link is `trench-companion.com/warband/detail/<id>`.
The JSON behind it is served without a login at
`synod.trench-companion.com/wp-json/synod/v1/warband/<id>`, an envelope
whose `warband_data` field is a JSON string. Its shape, measured on a
public warband on 25 September:

- Warband: `name`; `ducat_bank` and `glory_bank` (the founding
  allowance); `context` with `campaign_round`, `victory_points`,
  `failed_promotions`, and `stored_ratings` whose `rating_ducat` and
  `rating_glory` are the roster's value and `spare_ducat` and
  `spare_glory` the Strongbox; `debts`; `tags.tc_version`
  (`ver_default` is the Official rules); `equipment` (the stash);
  `fireteams`; `notes`.
- Faction: `faction.faction_property.object_id`, an id such as
  `fc_ironsultanate_fv_houseofwisdom`, the faction slug with an optional
  variant slug after `_fv_`.
- Exploration: `explorationskills[].object_id` (`es_` ids), `locations`.
- Each model: `purchase` (`cost_value`, `cost_type` where 0 is Ducats,
  the recruitment relation) and `model` with `name` (the variant's
  display name), `model` (an `md_` id), `elite`, `experience`,
  `list_skills` (`sk_` ids), `list_injury` (`in_` ids), `list_upgrades`
  (`up_` ids), `stat_selections`, `scar_reserves`, `recruited`, `active`,
  `notes`, and `equipment[]` each with a `purchase` (`purchaseid`, an
  `eq_` id, and the relation's price) and the equipment's `name`.

Their ids are slugs of the published names, and every object carries its
name, so resolution is **by name against our dataset**, the way the
NewRecruit import resolves by entry. Their ids are never stored.

**The change.**

1. **A server route** fetches the JSON on the user's action and returns
   it to the client; the browser never calls their host. Only the id is
   sent. A non-200, a missing `warband_data`, or a `warband_data` that is
   not JSON is an error the user sees, with the status (rule 2). Nothing
   is cached and nothing is fetched in bulk: this reads the user's own
   warband, once, when they paste the link.
2. **An importer** beside the NewRecruit one: `importTrenchCompanionWarband`
   takes the envelope and the dataset and returns a warband and a report,
   with the same `unmatched` contract. Faction and variant by name from
   the `fc_` id's two slugs; each model by `name` within that faction,
   then by the `md_` slug; each item by `equipment.name`; skills,
   injuries and upgrades by their `_` slugs against our names. Anything
   unresolved is listed in the report and left off the model, never
   guessed. `tc_version` other than `ver_default` is a warning naming the
   Preview rules.
3. **The ledger** is opened as `migrateFoundingPot` opens an imported
   NewRecruit warband: a founding credit of `ducat_bank` and `glory_bank`,
   one quartermaster debit of `rating_ducat` and `rating_glory`, leaving
   `spare_ducat` and `spare_glory` as the balance. Prices on the roster
   are ours (rule 1); where theirs differ, the report says so per item.
   A non-zero `debts` is a warning.
4. **Campaign state.** `experience`, `elite`, skills, injuries and upgrades
   land on the model as the NewRecruit import lands them;
   `failed_promotions` seeds the Promotion miss counter (FD-06b);
   `campaign_round` and `victory_points` are recorded on the warband and
   applied to a campaign only when the warband joins one. Exploration
   skills map by name; `locations` become discoveries (FD-07).
   `scar_reserves`, `stat_selections` and `active` are measured on the
   owner's own warband before they are mapped: their meaning is not
   stated anywhere public.
5. **The import screen** gains a Trench Companion field beside the
   NewRecruit upload, taking a link or a bare id.

**Test.** On a share link the owner supplies from a warband they own. The
public warband measured for this design belongs to a stranger and is not
committed. The test asserts the model count, each model's name and
entry, the equipment names, the Strongbox balance from `spare_ducat`, the
founding entry, the round, and the `unmatched` list exactly.

**Documentation.** `docs/NEWRECRUIT-IMPORT.md` gains a sibling section, or
a new sibling document, stating the endpoint, the
etiquette (the user's own link, on their action, nothing cached), and the
fields not yet mapped.

## SH-1. A public share page for a warband

**Why.** Trench Companion's share link is the thing people paste into the
group chat. Ours has nothing to paste.

**The change.**

1. **A share token.** A nullable, unique `shareToken` column on the synced
   warband record, set by a "Share" action in the builder and cleared by
   "Stop sharing". Random, not derived from the id. A local-only warband
   cannot be shared, and the builder says so: sharing needs the warband
   in the cloud.
2. **The page.** `/w/<token>` renders the Roster Sheet from FD-12 read
   only, with no builder controls, no session required, and a `noindex`
   header. An unknown or cleared token is a 404, not an empty sheet.
3. **The data.** The page reads the synced warband through the same
   loader the sync uses and renders it through the same components; no
   second rendering path.

**Test.** A shared warband renders at its token; the same warband after
"Stop sharing" is 404; a warband never shared is 404 for any token; the
page carries `noindex`. The migration is a pending one and is applied to
production under the standing authorisation in `CLAUDE.md`, and reported.

## RV-1. Per-warband ruleset conversion, with the report of what was lost

**Why.** Trench Companion lets a warband move between its Official and
Preview rules and tells the player what did not survive. TrenchLine has
two rulesets in the build and a switcher; a warband built under one is
read under whichever is current, silently.

**The change.**

1. **A warband records its ruleset** at creation (it may already; if not,
   `rulesetId` on the warband, durable).
2. **Convert** is an action in the builder: every unit, item and option is
   re-resolved by name in the target ruleset. The report lists each thing
   **kept**, **changed** (a price or a statline differs, with both
   values), and **lost** (no entry in the target). The player confirms or
   cancels; nothing changes before confirmation.
3. **Money.** A lost item is refunded at its recorded price as a
   `conversion` ledger entry; a price change moves no money, since the
   book does not re-charge a warband for a reprint. A lost model is
   refunded likewise and its Battlekit goes to the stash.
4. Opening a warband under a ruleset other than its own shows a bar
   offering to switch the app's ruleset or convert the warband, the way
   Trench Companion prompts.

**Test.** A warband converted between the two shipped rulesets reports
kept, changed and lost correctly for a fixture that has one of each; the
refunds book; the bar appears on a mismatch and not otherwise.

## Held for the demo

Not designed until the owner has shown the campaign side of Trench
Companion. Their shape, so the demo can confirm or overturn it:

- **GR-1, a shared game record with sign-off.** Both sides' post-battles
  hang off one record; the opponent confirms; a confirmed record is
  locked. The match id on `BattleSide` and the Chronicle are the record.
- **INV-1, campaign invites.** A campaign link a signed-in player joins,
  on top of the sync and the organiser that exist.
- **PGR-1, the linear reporter.** The wizard's steps presented as one
  numbered page, without changing what each step decides.
